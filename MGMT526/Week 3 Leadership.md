# Major Leadership Styles and Real-World Examples

- **Transformational Leadership**
    - **What it is:** Inspiring and motivating teams through a shared, compelling vision to drive major changes and innovation.
    - **Example:** [Steve Jobs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROPBxY_pbos) at Apple used his bold vision to push teams toward groundbreaking product development. [[1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROPBxY_pbos), [2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NY82yptNp5E), [3](https://www.creighton.edu/blog/8-most-common-leadership-styles)]

- **Autocratic (Authoritarian) Leadership**
    - **What it is:** Making decisions independently with little to no input from the group, ensuring fast execution and strict control.
    - **Example:** General George S. Patton used decisive, directive command in high-pressure military operations. [[1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IK8vJwIGuN4), [2](https://www.creighton.edu/blog/8-most-common-leadership-styles), [3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROPBxY_pbos)]

- **Democratic (Participative) Leadership**
    - **What it is:** Involving team members in the decision-making process, encouraging collaboration, and valuing diverse feedback.
    - **Example:** Indra Nooyi at PepsiCo relied on open dialogue and inclusive team engagement. [[1](https://www.creighton.edu/blog/8-most-common-leadership-styles), [2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROPBxY_pbos)]

- **Laissez-Faire (Delegative) Leadership**
    - **What it is:** A hands-off approach that grants high autonomy and decision-making freedom to skilled team members.
    - **Example:** Warren Buffett at Berkshire Hathaway provides managers broad independence to run their respective businesses. [[1](https://www.creighton.edu/blog/8-most-common-leadership-styles), [2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROPBxY_pbos)]

- **Servant Leadership**
    - **What it is:** Prioritizing the personal growth, well-being, and needs of team members above the leader's own agenda.
    - **Example:** Herb Kelleher at Southwest Airlines fostered high trust and loyalty by putting employees first. [[1](https://www.lindauerglobal.com/insight/common-leadership-styles/), [2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROPBxY_pbos), [3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NY82yptNp5E)]


## Week 3 Learning Notes: Leadership (Topic 4)

### 1. Why leadership matters

- **Individuals:** shapes motivation, growth, and job satisfaction. People don't quit companies, they quit managers.
- **Groups:** sets direction, resolves conflict, and builds cohesion.
- **Organizations:** drives culture, strategy execution, and performance.
- **Society:** leaders influence ethics, policy, and social change (and scandals like Enron show the downside).

### 2. What leadership is

The process of **influencing others** to willingly work toward shared goals. The key words are _influence_ and _willingly_. If people comply only because you can fire them, that's management with a bad attitude.

### 3. A quick history

- **Great Man era (pre-1900s):** leaders are born, not made.
- **Trait era (1900s-1940s):** hunt for the magic personality checklist.
- **Behavioral era (1940s-1960s):** it's what leaders _do_, not who they are.
- **Situational/contingency era (1960s-1980s):** it depends on the context.
- **Contemporary era (1980s-present):** vision, ethics, relationships, and followers.

### 4. Core theories

|Approach|Big idea|Key models|
|---|---|---|
|**Trait**|Leaders have distinguishing traits (intelligence, drive, integrity, self-confidence, emotional intelligence).|Great Man theory, Big Five|
|**Behavioral**|Leadership is learnable behavior, split into _task_ focus and _people_ focus.|Ohio State (initiating structure vs. consideration), Michigan (production- vs. employee-centered), Blake-Mouton Managerial Grid|
|**Situational**|Adapt your style to follower readiness.|Hersey-Blanchard: directing, coaching, supporting, delegating|
|**Contingency**|Effectiveness depends on a fit between leader, followers, and situation.|Fiedler's LPC model, House's Path-Goal, Vroom-Yetton decision model|

**Situational vs. contingency:** situational says _you_ flex your style. Fiedler's contingency view says styles are fairly fixed, so put the right leader in the right situation.

### 5. Power vs. influence

- **Power** is the _capacity_ to affect others' behavior. It's the loaded gun.
- **Influence** is the _actual process_ of changing attitudes or behavior (persuasion, consultation, coalition-building). It's the act of using it.
- Power creates the opportunity, but influence is how you get results. Leaders with lots of power and no influence skills get grudging compliance at best.

### 6. French & Raven's five bases of power

1. **Legitimate:** from your position or title ("I'm the boss").
2. **Reward:** control over raises, promotions, and perks.
3. **Coercive:** ability to punish (the fear-based one, with the shortest shelf life).
4. **Expert:** specialized knowledge or skill.
5. **Referent:** admiration and identification with the leader (charisma, respect).

**Takeaway:** expert and referent power (personal power) tend to earn commitment. Coercive power usually earns resentment and a polished résumé.

### 7. Contemporary approaches

- **Transformational:** inspires followers through vision and purpose, and develops them (idealized influence, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, individualized consideration).
- **Transactional:** exchanges rewards for performance, using contingent reward and management by exception.
- **Servant:** leader serves the team first.
- **Authentic:** self-aware, transparent, and values-driven.
- **Ethical:** models moral conduct and holds others accountable.
- **Charismatic:** relies on personal magnetism and emotional appeal.
- **Leader-Member Exchange (LMX):** quality of one-on-one relationships (in-group vs. out-group) drives outcomes.
- **Shared/distributed and adaptive leadership:** leadership spreads across the team and flexes with complex, changing environments.

### Exam-ready one-liner

Leadership has evolved from _"who leaders are"_ (traits) to _"what they do"_ (behaviors) to _"where they do it"_ (situation/contingency) to _"who they do it with and why"_ (contemporary approaches), all powered by influence built on the right mix of power bases.
