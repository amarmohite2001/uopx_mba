# MGT/526 Week 1 Discussion Summary
Management at Different Levels in an Organization (Dr. Gloden video reflection)

## Overall Theme
Nearly all classmates agree the four functions of management (planning, organizing,
leading, controlling) stay the same across levels but shift focus:
- Senior/top level: strategic, long-term, big-picture direction
- Middle level: translates strategy into actionable departmental plans
- Frontline/supervisory level: tactical, daily execution

Dr. Gloden's "altitude" metaphor was the recurring frame — senior leaders fly high
and set direction, middle managers navigate the path, frontline managers are on the
ground doing the work. Classmates backed this with examples from hospitality,
healthcare, pharmacy, telecom, retail, education, NASA, and athletics — largely
agreeing that strategic goals are meaningless without alignment and execution below.

## Competency Points Raised (beyond the four core functions)
- **Communication** — nearly universal mention; tied to preventing confusion, delays,
  duplicated work
- **Emotional intelligence** — senior leaders risk losing touch with frontline culture;
  needed to manage diverse personalities (Nazneen, Bryan, Akeria, Christyl, Fe, Shamica)
- **Adaptability/flexibility** — essential given shifting business conditions
  (Shaun, Hugo, Elizabeth, Dennis Gavin)
- **Problem-solving and decision-making** — core to translating strategy into workable
  solutions (Akira, Shamica, Nazneen, Elizabeth)
- **Conflict resolution / de-escalation** — an overlooked but critical skill since
  managers deal with people, not just numbers (Taunia, Shaun)
- **Accountability, delegation, constructive criticism** — framed around trust-building
  and skill development, not "dumping work" (Antoine, Bryan Boyd)
- **Employee input / bottom-up feedback loops** — frontline employees see problems
  senior leadership misses; involving them builds ownership and speeds change
  acceptance (Hugo, Shingo's "lookout on a ship" analogy, Akeria, Dennis, Dwan)
- **Time management, strategic thinking, technical literacy** — level-specific:
  strategic thinking more senior, technical/time-management more frontline
  (Bryan Boyd, Dwan Wilson)
