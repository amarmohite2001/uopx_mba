The four core functions of management are ==planning, organizing, leading, and controlling==. These steps work together as an ongoing process to help a business reach its goals. [1, 2]
## Planning
- Setting goals: Deciding what the business wants to achieve.
- Choosing steps: Finding the best actions to reach those goals.
- Making timelines: Setting schedules and budgets before starting. [1, 3, 4, 5]

## Organizing
- Arranging resources: Giving out tools, money, and materials.
- Assigning roles: Deciding who does which tasks.
- Building structure: Setting up teams and reporting lines. [1, 6, 7, 8]

## Leading
- Motivating teams: Inspiring workers to do their best work.
- Communicating clearly: Sharing the company vision and daily updates.
- Guiding people: Helping employees solve problems and grow. [4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10]

## Controlling
- Checking progress: Tracking work against the original plan.
- Measuring results: Comparing real outcomes to expected goals.
- Taking action: Fixing errors or changing course when things go wrong. [3, 4, 8, 11]

## Managerial Roles

In the 1960s, management scholar Henry Mintzberg, through direct observation of managers and other research, came up with three main types of managerial roles, with each of the roles including three or four sub-roles.

1. Interpersonal role
    
    - As part of this role, managers must interact with individuals both inside and outside the organization. Within the interpersonal role are figurehead, leader, and liaison sub-roles.
        
    - Sub-roles:
        
        1. Figurehead sub-role
            
            - Official representative of the organization or department
                
                - Gives awards, hosts parties, signs documents
                    
                - Responsible for setting the culture and ethics of the organization
                    
        2. Leader sub-role
            
            - Includes communicating, training, motivating, and redirecting constituents
                
        3. Liaison sub-role
            
            - Maintains communication channels, primarily with people outside of the organization
                
2. Informational role
    
    - This role includes being a monitor, disseminator, and spokesperson. Mintzberg thought this was the most important as accurate information is essential for good decision-making.
        
    - Sub-roles:
        
        1. Monitor sub-role
            
            - Gathers information from a variety of sources about anything that affects the company, including competition, technology changes, market changes, and new products and services
                
            - Must stay well-informed
                
        2. Disseminator sub-role
            
            - Forwards pertinent information to others, primarily within the organization, including subordinates
                
        3. Spokesperson sub-role
            
            - Transmits information to people outside the organization
                
3. Decisional role
    
    - This role involves being an entrepreneur, disturbance handler, resource allocator, and negotiator.
        
    - Sub-roles:
        
        1. Entrepreneur sub-role
            
            - An agent for change and innovation
                
            - Constantly scans the horizon, looking for new opportunities and solving existing problems
                
        2. Disturbance handler sub-role
            
            - Fixes problems between individuals or departments
                
        3. Resource allocator sub-role
            
            - Determines who gets what
                
        4. Negotiator sub-role
            
            - Formal representative of the organization with those outside the organization, such as vendors
