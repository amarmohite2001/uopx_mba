# Week 2: Strategic Planning

> **Focus:** How managers convert organizational objectives into actionable
> plans that build and sustain a competitive position.

## What this week builds

Week 2 extends Week 1 management foundations by focusing on planning. Effective
planning connects an organization's purpose, environment, resources, and
day-to-day work to its long-term objectives.

## Strategic planning process

1. **Clarify the mission and objectives.** Identify what the organization is
   trying to accomplish and why.
2. **Analyze the environment.** Assess internal capabilities and external
   conditions that could help or hinder success.
3. **Set goals.** Define the results the organization needs to achieve.
4. **Choose a strategy and plans.** Decide how the organization will compete
   and translate that choice into coordinated actions.
5. **Implement.** Assign owners, resources, timelines, and measures of
   success.
6. **Monitor and adapt.** Review results, learn from changes, and revise the
   plan when conditions require it.

Planning is continuous, not a one-time document. Managers adjust when
performance, customer needs, competitors, technology, or risks change.

## Goals and plans

### SMART goals

A useful goal is **SMART**:

| Element | Meaning | Question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Specific | Clearly states the intended result | What exactly will change? |
| Measurable | Can be evaluated with evidence | How will progress be tracked? |
| Achievable | Is realistic with available capabilities | Can the organization do this? |
| Relevant | Supports an important objective | Why does this matter now? |
| Time-bound | Has a deadline or review period | By when must it happen? |

Example: "Increase repeat online purchases by 10% within the next two
quarters by improving the post-purchase support experience."

### Types of plans

| Plan type | Purpose | Typical horizon |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic plan | Sets organization-wide direction and competitive priorities | Long term |
| Tactical plan | Converts strategy into departmental or functional actions | Medium term |
| Operational plan | Directs routine work, schedules, procedures, and near-term targets | Short term |
| Contingency plan | Defines a response if a known risk or assumption changes | Trigger-based |
| Crisis management plan | Coordinates action during a serious disruption or emergency | Immediate response |

Contingency planning prepares for plausible changes before they happen. Crisis
management focuses on protecting people, operations, and communication when a
high-impact event is already occurring.

## Environmental analysis

### SWOT analysis

**SWOT** organizes information about an organization and its environment.

| Internal | External |
|---|---|
| **Strengths:** capabilities or assets that help the organization | **Opportunities:** favorable trends or openings to pursue |
| **Weaknesses:** gaps or limitations that reduce effectiveness | **Threats:** outside pressures or risks that could harm performance |

Managers use SWOT to prioritize which strengths to use, weaknesses to improve,
opportunities to pursue, and threats to reduce or prepare for.

### VRIO framework

**VRIO** tests whether a resource or capability can create competitive
advantage:

| Question | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Valuable | Does it help the organization exploit an opportunity or reduce a threat? |
| Rare | Do few competitors have it? |
| Inimitable | Is it difficult or costly for competitors to copy? |
| Organized | Is the organization set up to capture its value? |

A capability is more likely to support a sustained advantage when the answer
to all four questions is yes. A resource has little strategic value if the
organization cannot organize people, processes, and systems around it.

### Porter's Five Forces

Porter's model examines the pressures that shape industry competition and
profit potential:

1. **Competitive rivalry:** intensity of competition among existing firms.
2. **Threat of new entrants:** ease with which new competitors can enter.
3. **Bargaining power of buyers:** customers' ability to demand lower prices or
   better terms.
4. **Bargaining power of suppliers:** suppliers' ability to raise costs or
   constrain access to inputs.
5. **Threat of substitutes:** availability of different products or services
   that meet the same customer need.

Stronger forces generally make it harder for organizations in an industry to
earn attractive returns. Managers use this analysis to identify where they can
reduce pressure or differentiate their offer.

## Competitive strategy

| Strategy | Core idea |
|---|---|
| Cost leadership | Deliver comparable value at a lower cost or price. |
| Differentiation | Deliver distinctive value customers are willing to choose or pay more for. |
| Focus | Serve a narrow customer segment especially well through cost leadership or differentiation. |

A strategy works when it matches the organization's goals, capabilities, and
competitive environment. Choosing a strategy also means making tradeoffs about
what the organization will not prioritize.

## Turning strategy into execution

Managers make strategy real by:

- Breaking strategic goals into tactical and operational work.
- Assigning clear accountability and decision authority.
- Allocating people, budget, technology, and time to chosen priorities.
- Communicating why the strategy matters and how each team contributes.
- Tracking milestones, key performance indicators, and regular reviews.
- Updating plans when evidence shows that assumptions are no longer valid.

## AI in strategic development

AI can help managers process large amounts of information, identify patterns,
model scenarios, summarize market signals, and generate initial strategic
options. It can accelerate analysis, but it does not replace managerial
judgment or accountability.

Managers should check AI-supported recommendations for data quality, bias,
privacy, security, feasibility, and alignment with organizational values before
acting on them.

## Applied example

A regional coffee company wants to grow recurring revenue.

1. It sets a SMART goal: increase subscription revenue by 15% within 12
   months.
2. Its SWOT analysis identifies a strong local brand as a strength and large
   national delivery services as a threat.
3. Its VRIO analysis finds that local roaster partnerships are valuable, rare,
   and difficult to copy, but its online ordering process is not organized to
   use that advantage.
4. Its Five Forces analysis shows strong rivalry and many substitutes.
5. It chooses a focused differentiation strategy: subscriptions built around
   exclusive local-roaster products and personalized delivery.
6. It creates a contingency plan for supplier disruption and tracks new
   subscriptions, renewal rate, fulfillment cost, and customer satisfaction.

## Review questions

2. How does a SMART goal improve an otherwise vague objective?
3. What is the difference between a contingency plan and a crisis management
   plan?
4. How do SWOT and VRIO answer different planning questions?
5. How can Porter's Five Forces influence a competitive strategy choice?
6. What should a manager validate before using AI-generated strategic advice?
